Life has been sending signals
for 3.8 billion years.
We're only now learning to receive them.

In Mozambique, a bird called the honeyguide does something remarkable. It flies to a person, calls, waits — then leads them, step by step, to a beehive. The human takes the honey and leaves the wax. Signaling, navigation, payment. A full collaboration protocol between two species — running for generations.

Cross-species communication has always been productive. It was just handcrafted, one relationship at a time. We're not here to translate animal language. We're here to build the open tools — sensors, networks, AI — that let every species and every ecosystem finally be heard.

Why Now
🤝
Cross-species collaboration is nothing new.

The honeyguide leading humans to honey. Dolphins herd fish toward waiting fishermen in Brazil — a partnership passed down for generations. Lumbering elephants guided by a single command. These aren't dreams. They're working protocols — handcrafted, one relationship at a time, impossible to scale.

📡
Signals are everywhere. We've been missing most of them.

A tiger's stripes are a barcode. A bird's call carries identity, mood, intent. A pond's water chemistry tells you if it's thriving or dying. Animals and ecosystems have been broadcasting data for millions of years — we just didn't have the receivers.

⚡
Now, for the first time, we can listen at scale.

Edge AI, open-source hardware, and low-power mesh networks have crossed a threshold. A camera the size of a matchbox can identify individual animals. A sensor no bigger than your thumb can monitor water quality for a year on solar power. The pieces are ready. What was missing was someone connecting them into a replicable system.

🔧
We don't speak for animals. We build the tools that let them be heard.

Seeed is not a research lab. We're an open-hardware platform — turning the ability to receive signals from any species, in any environment, into something any researcher, any NGO, any Fab Lab can replicate and deploy.

What We Do

Six Research Areas,
One Platform

Every animal study — whether it's counting tigers in Bhutan or monitoring water quality in Hong Kong — starts with the same question: how do we receive the signals that are already there? Here's how open hardware changes what's possible, across six core research areas.

Individual Identification & Lifecycle Tracking

The old way

Mark-recapture, photo ID, GPS collars — invasive, expensive, every image reviewed by hand.

What's changed

AI reads natural markings — tiger stripes, fin notches — like barcodes. Identify without capture, track without collars.

CV Edge AI

Population Counting & Dynamics

The old way

Camera traps + manual review. A single population estimate takes weeks. Data is often outdated by the time you get it.

What's changed

On-device AI identifies and counts species automatically. Results return via low-power mesh — no SD card hikes, no months of waiting.

CV LoRa

Cognitive Decline & Aging Research

The old way

Manual ethogram coding — short observation windows, subjective, expert-dependent. Subtle declines go unnoticed until they're severe.

What's changed

24/7 gait and vocal monitoring. VLM detects limping and vocal changes before they're visible to the human eye — early warning, not post-mortem.

CV VLM STT

Acoustic Monitoring

The old way

Record + send to experts for manual listening. Expensive, slow, impossible to scale beyond a handful of sites.

What's changed

Edge-based sound classification identifies species, behavior, and stress from calls — automatically, year-round, with audio-visual cross-validation to filter out wind and rain noise.

STT Audio AI VLM

Social Behavior & Group Dynamics

The old way

Years of focal observation and handwritten notes. Patterns like ostracism or bond dissolution take seasons to spot.

What's changed

Multi-camera arrays automatically identify every individual and map their interactions — building a social network graph that updates in real time.

CV Multi-modal Edge Computing

Habitat & Carrying Capacity

The old way

Periodic field sampling + lab analysis. A wetland can change dramatically between visits — and you miss almost everything.

What's changed

Continuous water quality, weather, and soil sensors report daily. Restoration progress becomes something you watch, not something you wait for.
