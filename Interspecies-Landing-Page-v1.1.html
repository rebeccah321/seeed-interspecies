Technology for
Interspecies Understanding

In Mozambique, a bird called the honeyguide recruits humans to beehives. It calls, they follow, and they share the honey. A partnership that has worked for generations — between two species that never learned each other's language, but developed a protocol anyway.
This isn't a fairytale. It's a documented biological partnership. Cross-species communication has always been productive — it was just handcrafted, one relationship at a time, hard to scale.
That's what changes now. We're not talking to animals in their language. We're here to build the receivers — sensors, networks, AI — the open tools that let every species and every ecosystem finally be heard.

WHY

Cross-species collaboration is nothing new.

The honeyguide leads humans to honey. Dolphins herd fish toward waiting fishermen in Brazil. These partnerships have run for centuries — but always one relationship at a time, master to apprentice, impossible to scale.

Signals are everywhere. We've been missing most of them.

Calls, gaits, stripe patterns, water quality, temperature — animals and ecosystems have been broadcasting data for millions of years. Until now, we could receive only a tiny fraction.

Now, for the first time, we can listen at scale.

Edge AI, open-source hardware, and low-power mesh networks have crossed a threshold. For the first time, listening at scale is possible.

We don't speak for animals. We build the tools that let them be heard.

Seeed is not a lab that speaks for animals. We build the open-source hardware that lets any species, any ecosystem, finally be heard.
